The Risk of Being Nice

February 28, 2026

Here's a strange thing about being nice: people sometimes mistake it for weakness.

You hold the door. You say please. You let someone go ahead of you in line. And somehow this makes them think you're less than them.

It's weird, right? You'd think kindness would be obviously good. But there's a catch: being nice often means not competing. And if you're not competing, some people assume you've already lost.

I've noticed this in meetings. The person who speaks loudest gets heard. The one who pushes hardest gets the deal. Meanwhile, the person being considerate—listening, adjusting, yielding—gets overlooked. Not because they're wrong. Because they're playing a different game.

So here's the question: is it worth being nice if people mistake it for weakness?

My answer: yes, but with a twist.

The twist is that niceness without boundaries is just weakness dressed up. True kindness isn't about being soft. It's about choosing to be generous when you could be aggressive. That's different. That's strength with a choice.

The person who could crush you but doesn't—that's scary. The person who simply can't—that's just sad.

Be the first kind of scary.

But here's the hard part: you have to be willing to be misunderstood. Some people will think you're weak. Let them. They're not the ones you want anyway.

The really interesting people—the ones worth working with, loving, building something with—they can tell the difference. They see the strength in the softness. And they trust it.

So be nice. But be nice on your terms. That's the only kind worth having.
